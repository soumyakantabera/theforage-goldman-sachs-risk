## Scenario 1: Getting to a 9:00 a.m. Monday Morning Exam

**Risks Identified (from table + expanded):**

1. **A transit strike affecting public transportation (Market Risk)**  
   External systemic shock. High impact on timely arrival.  
   *Mitigation:* Identify backup transportation (rideshare, bike, carpool) 48 hours in advance; subscribe to real-time alert apps.

2. **A friend forgetting to bring their extra calculator (Credit Risk)**  
   Counterparty dependency failure. Low-to-moderate impact if exam allows sharing.  
   *Mitigation:* Bring two calculators yourself; confirm with friend the night before via text.

3. **Not having enough balance on a public transport card (Liquidity Risk)**  
   Insufficient liquid resources at the point of need. High impact if no cash alternative.  
   *Mitigation:* Top up card with 20% buffer the evening prior; carry small cash reserve.

4. **Misplacing the exam hall ticket (Operational Risk)**  
   Internal process or documentation failure. High impact (potential denial of entry).  
   *Mitigation:* Digital photo backup on phone + physical copy in wallet and backpack.

5. **Misinterpreting the exam timetable (Model Risk)**  
   Incorrect data interpretation or assumption error. High impact (missed exam).  
   *Mitigation:* Cross-check official university source twice; set three calendar reminders.

**Additional Risks:** Oversleeping (phone alarm failure — Operational), sudden illness (Health/Operational), traffic accident en route (Market/Operational).

## Scenario 2: Organizing a Weekend Road Trip with Friends

1. **A friend not paying their share of the trip expenses (Credit Risk)**  
   Counterparty default on verbal agreement. High impact on group budget.  
   *Mitigation:* Written cost-sharing agreement signed before booking; collect funds upfront via payment app.

2. **Car breakdown (Operational Risk)**  
   Vehicle reliability or maintenance failure. High impact on trip continuity.  
   *Mitigation:* Pre-trip mechanic inspection + roadside assistance subscription + backup rental car plan.

3. **Overestimating the car's fuel efficiency (Model Risk)**  
   Inaccurate forecasting model. Moderate impact on budget.  
   *Mitigation:* Use conservative EPA estimates minus 15%; budget extra fuel cost.

4. **Fuel prices suddenly increased (Market Risk)**  
   External commodity price volatility. Moderate impact.  
   *Mitigation:* Fill tank before departure; use price-comparison apps to refuel at cheapest stations.

5. **Running out of money during the trip (Liquidity Risk)**  
   Cash flow shortfall mid-trip. High impact on group dynamics.  
   *Mitigation:* Per-person spending caps + emergency cash pool contributed by all.

**Additional Risks:** Bad weather forcing major changes (Market), interpersonal conflict over itinerary (Operational/Reputational), lost wallet or phone (Operational).

## Scenario 3: Planning an On-Campus Event for an Extracurricular Organization

1. **Failure of audiovisual equipment (Operational Risk)**  
   Technology or vendor failure. High impact on event quality.  
   *Mitigation:* Full equipment test 24 hours prior; backup projector/laptop on site + IT contact on call.

2. **An overlapping major campus event reducing attendance (Market Risk)**  
   Competitive external event. High impact on turnout and revenue.  
   *Mitigation:* Check master university calendar 4–6 weeks ahead; co-promote or adjust date if conflict found.

3. **A sponsor backing out at the last minute (Credit Risk)**  
   Sponsor default on commitment. High impact on budget.  
   *Mitigation:* Written sponsor agreements with 30-day notice clause; secure 2–3 backup sponsors or self-funding reserve.

4. **Unexpected costs exceeding the budget (Enterprise Risk)**  
   Budget overrun affecting organization finances. High strategic impact.  
   *Mitigation:* 15–20% contingency line item; require dual approval for any spend >10% of budget.

5. **Overestimating attendee numbers based on social media interest (Model Risk)**  
   Flawed demand forecasting. Moderate-to-high impact on catering/venue.  
   *Mitigation:* Require RSVPs 7 days prior; use historical event data; plan scalable food options.

**Additional Risks:** Low turnout due to poor marketing (Operational), safety or security incident (Enterprise/Reputational), key organizer sudden illness (Operational).

## Key Takeaways — Goldman Sachs Risk Culture

The three scenarios illustrate the exact risk taxonomy Goldman Sachs Operations Analysts apply daily:

- **Market Risk**: External shocks (transit strike, fuel prices, competing events)
- **Credit Risk**: Counterparty or sponsor default (friend not paying, sponsor backing out)
- **Operational Risk**: Process or technology failure (misplaced ticket, AV failure, car breakdown)
- **Liquidity Risk**: Cash shortfall at critical moment (transport card balance, running out of money)
- **Model Risk**: Incorrect assumptions or forecasts (timetable misread, fuel efficiency overestimation, attendance overestimation)
- **Enterprise Risk**: Broader strategic or reputational impact (budget overrun affecting club finances)

Thorough risk identification, categorization, and documented mitigation planning is the foundation of operational resilience — whether protecting a student’s exam grade or safeguarding billions in client assets and firm capital at Goldman Sachs.
